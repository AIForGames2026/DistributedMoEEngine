# RDMA transport over HCA loopback (2026-11-05)

TASK-07 acceptance run. `dispatch` (ReleaseFast, `-Drdma=true -Dcpu=native`) on `gx10-03`, two processes on one ConnectX-7 port (`roceP2p1s0f0`, RoCE v2, GID index 3, MTU 4096), each with its own Reliable Connection queue pair. Every data packet is one `IBV_WR_RDMA_WRITE_WITH_IMM` of 7,552 bytes into the peer's registered ring; credits and goodbyes are zero-length writes with an immediate. Queue-pair details are swapped over TCP before connecting. All packets are checked for CRC32C, slot generation, and contents, as on the other backends.

Co-tenant on `gx10-03`: vLLM resident, GPU 0%, load average 0.2 to 0.4.

## Against `perftest` (same session, same port, 7,552-byte messages)

| Measure | `perftest` | Target (Plan TASK-07) | Ours | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-way latency, p50 | `ib_write_lat` typical 3.12 us | at most 1.5x = 4.68 us | 4.34 to 4.39 us (3 runs of 100,000 round trips; p99 4.94 to 5.02 us) | **pass** |
| One-direction bandwidth | `ib_write_bw` 102.4 to 103.6 Gb/s (median 102.65) | at least 90% = 92.4 Gb/s | 100.7 to 102.0 Gb/s (5 runs of 200,000 packets, 256 slots) | **pass** |

Our latency is `dispatch --latency`: rank 0 sends one dispatch and waits for the combine, and reports half the round trip, as `ib_write_lat` does. It includes sealing and checking each packet (CRC32C, about 0.3 us per pass on Grace) and the copy into the send buffer, which `perftest` does not do. Bandwidth is `dispatch --oneway`: a stream of dispatches with one combine at the end, like `ib_write_bw`.

`perftest`'s default "MB/sec" is MiB/s (12,340 "MB/sec" = 103.5 Gb/s); the numbers above use `--report_gbits`.

## Ring size sets the bandwidth

The sender can only have one ring's worth of packets in flight before the receiver's credits come back, so a small ring stalls the link. Same run, 5 times per size:

| Slots per ring | Ring size | One-direction Gb/s | Polls waiting for credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 0.5 MB | 74.2 to 82.8 | 0.9 to 1.3 million |
| 128 | 1.0 MB | 90.7 to 93.5 | 0.2 to 0.4 million |
| 256 | 1.9 MB | 89.5 to 102.1 (4 of 5 at 96 or more) | 0.2 million |
| 512 | 3.9 MB | 98.2 to 102.1 | under 0.15 million |

The waits show the receiver side and credit round trip are the limit, not the sender's copy into its send buffer. The `rdma` modes therefore default to 256 slots; shared memory and TCP keep 64 so earlier results stay comparable.

## Design notes

- **Receive side is zero-copy**: the NIC writes each packet straight into its slot in the receiver's ring, and the receiver reads it there. The immediate arrives in a receive completion after the data has landed, which is the readiness signal.
- **Send side copies once** into a registered send buffer (one per slot). A send buffer is reused only after the send that used it has completed (`tf_rdma_write_imm` waits on its completion), so no payload is overwritten while the NIC may still be reading it. Building packets directly in the send buffer would remove the copy; the measurements above show it is not the bottleneck today.
- **Ordered teardown** follows the PoC finding: after `Endpoint.finish` (rank 0 says goodbye first), each side waits for its sends to complete, rank 0 closes the bootstrap socket, and rank 1 destroys its queue pair only after seeing that close.
- Every send is signaled; with all completions in order on one queue pair, one counter tracks which send buffers are free.
