# Final benchmarks on one Spark (2026-11-21)

Every figure for TASK-19's whitepaper that one machine can give, measured on `gx10-02` at one commit, `f3873c4`, from Nov 21 04:22 to Nov 22 04:30 UTC. The cross-machine figures (three Sparks over RDMA, four machines with the desktop) need `gx10-01` and `gx10-03`, which were busy, so they stay with the Nov 11, 16 and 20 runs.

## Setup

- **Machine:** `gx10-02`, NVIDIA GB10 (Cortex-X925, 20 cores, 121 GB shared memory), kernel 7.0.0-1019-nvidia, driver 580.178.04, Zig 0.17.0. NVMe `ESL01TBTLCZ-27J2-TYN`, ext4, 78% full. Nothing else ran on it (GPU 0% before the start).
- **Build:** `f3873c4`, ReleaseFast, `-Drdma=true -Dgpu=true`, into its own prefix (`zig-out-final`).
- **Scripts:**
  - `2026-11-21/run.sh` runs the seven blocks, each to its own folder.
  - `fixup.sh` reruns the cache runs that needed `--max-step`, and `glm-golden.sh` reruns GLM's golden check.
  - `followup.sh` and `loader2.sh` look into two questions the blocks raised.
  - `progress.txt` has every block's start and end.
- **Unit tests:** 139 of 139 pass (`setup/unit-tests.log`). The "failed command" line there is stderr output from the split tests; the build summary says all passed.
- **GPU formats:** every weight format's GPU kernels agree with the CPU within 1e-3 (`setup/gpu-formats.log`).

## Correctness

- **Bit for bit against Nov 20 (TASK-42's `record.sh compare`):** 58 of 58 runs match. That covers the eight tiny models and seven real ones, each on the CPU, the GPU, split three ways and with lookahead, and GLM-5.3-Flash through the expert cache.
- **Against transformers:** every golden passes with f32 activations:
  - OLMoE, Qwen3 (YaRN 4), Qwen3.6, Qwen1.5-MoE, Mixtral, gpt-oss and Moonlight, plus the tiny Qwen2-MoE, Mixtral, DeepSeek V3 and gpt-oss models.
  - GLM-5.3-Flash passes with `--max-step 4096`, as in TASK-32, with its known near-tie excused (`glm-golden/`).
- **fp8 activations miss transformers' bar:** the Qwen2-MoE split (`correct/qwen2moe/`) and the Qwen3 split four ways, over shared memory and over RDMA (`transport/`). This is the known fp8 result from Nov 20 (`../olmoe/2026-11-20-shared-parts.md`), not a new failure.

**CPU only:** OLMoE decodes at 5.4 tokens/s and Qwen3 Q8_0 at 1.3, at 512 tokens of context (2 runs each).

**gpt-oss and Moonlight run attention on the CPU.** Their logs say "GPU attention off", and attention takes most of their time: 185 of gpt-oss's 255 s at 4k, and 19 of Moonlight's 20 s of decode at 4k. gpt-oss took 75 minutes per 16k prompt. Moving these attention variants (sinks with a sliding window, and MLA) to the GPU is TASK-54.

**Qwen3.6's hybrid attention holds up at length.** Its decode falls 11% from 512 to 16k, against 41% for Qwen3. Most of its layers are Gated DeltaNet, whose state does not grow with the context.

### Split four ways on the same GPU

Shared memory, one machine, 512 tokens of context, one run each:

| Model | One process | fp8 split | f32 split |
|---|---|---|---|
| OLMoE | 96.7 decode / 2,088 prefill | 42.0 / 200 | 49.0 / 779 |
| Qwen3 Q8_0 | 36.8 / 772 | 14.5 / 69 | 17.0 / 352 |
| Qwen3.6 | 32.8 / 584 | 15.6 / 83 | 17.5 / 338 |
| Mixtral | 16.0 / 270 | 12.1 / 130 | 13.1 / 210 |

On one machine the split only costs: four processes share one GPU, and each layer waits for all of them. fp8 saves bytes on a real link. Over shared memory it only adds the conversion, which costs most in prefill. Today's numbers say nothing about fp8 over a cable; the three-Spark runs do.

## Drive and loader

| Read | Rate |
|---|---|
| `fio`, 1 MiB sequential, queue depth 16, O_DIRECT, io_uring (16 GiB file, 3 runs) | 7.34 to 7.35 GB/s |
| `fio`, 4 KiB random, queue depth 32 | 311,000 IOPS (1.27 GB/s), p99 181 µs |
| The engine's reader (`uring_bench`), 1 MiB, queue depth 8 to 64 | 6.84 to 6.97 GB/s |
| `fio` on the model files themselves | 6.95 (OLMoE), 6.79 GB/s (Qwen3) |
| `tfload`, half the experts, the first time | 5.41 to 5.50 GB/s |
| `tfload`, the same half again | 6.83 to 6.90 GB/s |

**The loader reaches 94% of `fio` once a file is not in the page cache.** In the storage block every load was the first read of that file after the speed block, and all of them ran at about 5.45 GB/s. In the follow-up the first load of each file ran at 5.4 GB/s and the next two at 6.85 to 6.90. The Nov 5 build of `tfload` (`9e39d09`) gives the same numbers, so nothing regressed. Nov 5 saw the same pattern on `gx10-03`: "7.0 after the first two runs".

The likely cause: the speed block had just read these files through the page cache, and an O_DIRECT read must first drop those cached pages. This is an inference from the pattern; we cannot drop the page cache without root to confirm it. Both model files are in 36 to 49 extents, so fragmentation is not the cause.

## Expert cache and lookahead

**GLM-5.3-Flash, 170 GB, more than the machine's memory:** 512 tokens of context, 64 generated, one run each.

| Cache | Decode | Decode, lookahead 2 | Already loaded | Loaded in time by a hint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 slots (14 GB) | 1.40 tokens/s | 1.49 | 15.4% | 7.4% |
| 2,500 (35 GB) | 1.85 | 1.83 | 22.1% | 7.6% |
| 4,000 (57 GB) | 2.39 | 2.23 | 26.5% | 7.1% |
| 5,500 (78 GB) | 2.86 | 2.67 | 28.7% | 6.5% |

**Qwen3-30B-A3B through a cache a quarter and a half of its experts' size:** 128 generated tokens.

| Cache | Decode | Decode, lookahead 2 | Prefill, without and with | Already loaded | Loaded in time by a hint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,536 slots (25%) | 9.87 tokens/s | 10.58 | 85 / 108 tokens/s | 74.5% | 4.8% |
| 3,072 (50%) | 18.00 | 20.30 | 96 / 109 | 85.1% | 3.3% |

- **Lookahead loads about 3 to 8% of calls in time**, and makes prefill 5% faster on GLM and 13 to 27% faster on Qwen3.
- **It helps decode only where misses are rare enough for hints to keep up:** by 7 to 13% on Qwen3, but on GLM only at the smallest cache. At the larger GLM caches decode is 1 to 7% slower with it, as its loads compete with the misses for the drive.
- **The hit-rate columns are not comparable between the two settings:** with lookahead the "already loaded" share drops, because hinted experts evict others.

**Compact formats against transformers:** as on Nov 16, bf16 and fp8 report how far they move rather than meeting 1e-2. Two prompts give different counts than Nov 16: OLMoE bf16's third prompt matches 18 of 64 tokens (64 then), and Qwen3 fp8's third matches 32 of 32 (2 then).

**The follow-up traced this to Nov 18.** It built eight commits from TASK-27 to today and reran both checks on each (`followup/kv/summary.txt`).
- `b05d5fb`, `21172ab` and `14cf7b8` give Nov 16's counts. `5449a56` and every later commit give today's.
- Between `14cf7b8` and `5449a56` lie `4143dee` (the live view) and the two commits titled "Make cached GPU expert runs repeatable bit for bit" (`4c98261`, `5449a56`).
- Those two changed how GPU expert outputs are combined. That fits a near-tie falling the other way, but the follow-up did not build the commits in between, so which one did it is not settled. The f32 goldens pass either way.

## Transport on one machine

`dispatch` with 4,096 dispatch and 4,096 combine packets, 64 slots, 5 runs each (medians). RDMA through `rocep1s0f0` loops back in the NIC; nothing crosses a cable.

| Path | Per token, pipelined | Both directions | One way: min / p50 / p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared memory | 2.57 µs | 47.0 Gb/s | 1.24 / 1.50 / 1.85 µs |
| RDMA, one process | 2.85 µs | 42.4 Gb/s | 4.11 / 4.42 / 4.72 µs |
| RDMA, two processes | 3.07 µs | 39.4 Gb/s | 4.25 / 4.61 / 4.89 µs |
| TCP, 127.0.0.1 | 12.18 µs | 9.9 Gb/s | 4.29 / 4.72 / 7.20 µs |

Over loopback TCP's latency matches RDMA's, but its pipelined throughput is a quarter of RDMA's. The engine split four ways over RDMA passes Qwen3's golden with f32 activations, like the same split over shared memory.
