# Swarm tracker on two Sparks (TASK-09), Nov 11

The tracker (`src/tracker.zig`) ran on `gx10-02`. Engine rank 0 (router) ran on `gx10-02` and rank 1 (expert, experts 32 to 63) on `gx10-03`, linked by RDMA over their direct cable. A `tracker watch` process on `gx10-03` stood in for a second peer on the other node. Script, versions, and every log are in [`2026-11-11/`](2026-11-11/).

Settings: heartbeat every 250 ms, unhealthy after 1,000 ms without one, OLMoE-1B-7B Q8_0, F32 payload, `--gpu`. Both Sparks were NTP-synced (offsets 1.9 ms and 0.5 ms), so timestamps from different machines are good to a few milliseconds.

## Acceptance

| Check | Target | Result |
|---|---|---|
| New process's experts in every peer's map | under 2 s | 2 ms in the engine run; bench p50 3.4 ms, max 10.9 ms (16 peers on another node) |
| Killed process marked unhealthy | under 5 s | 52 ms in the engine run; bench p50 22.3 ms, max 29.0 ms |
| Stopped (hung) process marked unhealthy | under 5 s | 0.79 s in the engine run; bench p50 1,014 ms, max 1,024 ms |
| Dispatcher refuses an unhealthy process | refuses | every time: mid-run after a kill and after a stop, and at startup (waits instead of connecting) |

## Engine runs over RDMA

All times are UTC. The signal time is taken on `gx10-03` just before the signal.

**Join.** The router started first and waited. The expert joined at 11:54:08.527. The router listed it at 08.529 and the watcher on `gx10-03` at 08.527. The router took the expert's 32 announced experts as the placement, with no `--host` and no placement file. It passed the prompt checks (logits within 4.01e-5) and the generation check (every token matches transformers' `generate()`). It then generated at 54.7 to 55.7 tokens/s, the same as TASK-15b's 54.4.

**Killed expert.** SIGKILL at 11:55:09.537. The tracker marked it unhealthy at 09.589, when its connection closed without a goodbye, and the watcher saw the change at 09.590. The router refused at 09.600, 10 ms after the map arrived, after 244,702 expert calls:

```
rdma: refusing to route to rank 1 (tracker id 115, pid 760934 on GX10-03): unhealthy in map v306, received 10 ms ago
```

RDMA itself gives no error when the peer dies. Without the tracker, the router would wait forever for combines that never come.

**Stopped expert.** SIGSTOP at 11:56:17.469. The process stays alive and its sockets stay open, so only the missing heartbeats show it. The tracker marked it unhealthy at 18.263 (0.79 s), and the router refused at 18.273.

**Router started while the only expert was stopped.** The router joined at 11:56:50.856 and found no healthy expert, so it waited. SIGCONT went to the expert at 55.661, the tracker marked it healthy again at 55.665, and the router connected at 55.670 and passed. It routed nothing to the stopped process during those 4.8 s.

## Bench

`tracker bench` opens 16 watcher connections. Each round, it then joins a fake expert and waits until all 16 maps show it, drops the expert's connection (as a kill would), and joins another that stops its heartbeats (as a stop would). It runs 20 rounds and times everything on one clock.

| Where the 16 peers ran | Join seen by all | Drop seen by all | Stalled heartbeat seen by all |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gx10-03`, tracker over the direct cable | p50 3.4 ms, max 10.9 ms | p50 22.3 ms, max 29.0 ms | p50 1,014 ms, max 1,024 ms |
| `gx10-02`, tracker on loopback | p50 1.4 ms, max 5.7 ms | p50 21.6 ms, max 36.2 ms | p50 1,019 ms, max 1,030 ms |

Most of the drop time is the bench client's own shutdown: its thread checks for a stop flag every 20 ms. In the engine run, the tracker saw the killed process within 52 ms.

## Found and fixed during the run

- **"Tracker full" on the first attempt.** Processes that left or died stay in the map for 30 s, so peers can see them as unhealthy. The first bench's 40 departed fake experts, plus the second bench's 16 watchers, filled all 64 slots. The tracker now drops the process that went down longest ago when it needs room (33 times in the run above).
- **Signal times in the first attempt were early.** They came from a separate ssh call, so they ran ahead of the actual signal by the ssh setup time. The script now logs the time in the same command as the signal.
- **Slow exit after a refusal.** The router used to spend 5 s waiting for the stopped peer to close its side before exiting. It now closes at once after a refusal.

## Limits

- The tracker is a single point of failure, as Lumabri's is. If a router loses its tracker connection, it counts every peer as unhealthy and stops routing, since it can no longer check health.
- A heartbeat proves the process is scheduled, not that it is making progress. A rank whose main thread deadlocks while its tracker thread runs would stay healthy.
- The engine still runs two ranks. The tracker lists any number of processes (up to 64) and their experts, but a router connects to one expert process. Replicas and failing over to another holder need more than two ranks.
- There is no authentication. The tracker is meant for a trusted lab network, as the direct cable and Tailscale are.
