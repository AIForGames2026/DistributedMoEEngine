# GLM-5.3-Flash: accuracy investigation and the two-Spark windows (TASK-32, 2026-11-17 to 20)

Work in progress. This records what the real-model golden check found, how the difference from transformers was narrowed down, and what happened in the two-Spark windows on the night of Nov 17 and the morning of Nov 20. The model, its GGUF and its acceptance criteria are described under TASK-32 in `Plan.md`. Logs and scripts are in `2026-11-17-glm53/`, the investigation's in `2026-11-17-glm53/accuracy/`.

## The golden check

The reference is transformers 5.23's `Glm5NextForConditionalGeneration` in float32 on the GGUF's dequantized weights, loaded lazily (`tools/ref/ref_glm53.py --lazy`, about 10 hours on the desktop's CPU with 32 generated tokens per prompt; `accuracy/reference.log`). It holds three short prompts (5, 17 and 15 tokens) and one of 2,600 tokens, past DSA's 2,048-token budget.

One process on `gx10-02`, experts streamed from NVMe (`--gpu --cache 5000`, `accuracy/golden-local.log`):

| Prompt | Logits max error | Top-1 | Expert choices shared | DSA pools shared | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (5 tokens) | 2.18e-3 | 5 of 5 | 100% | (none selected) | ok |
| 1 (17 tokens) | 5.05e-2 | 17 of 17 | 5,710 of 5,712 | (none selected) | FAIL |
| 2 (15 tokens) | 2.27e-3 | 15 of 15 | 100% | (none selected) | ok |
| 3 (2,600 tokens) | 3.88 (not held to 1e-2) | 32 of 32 | 97.242% (target 99.5%) | 99.708% (target 99.9%) | FAIL |

- **Prompt 1** fails on 2 flipped router choices (layers 40 and 41, token 8). The engine's own gap between its 8th and 9th expert there is 5.6e-5, larger than the 1e-6 near-tie rule of TASK-31, so it is not excused.
- **The long prompt's** first DSA layers (3 and 7) choose exactly transformers' pools, and every top-1 token matches.
- **Generation** (`accuracy/golden-gen.log`, the golden check now runs it after a forward mismatch): prompts 0 and 2 give all 32 tokens of `generate()` with logits within 4e-4. Prompt 1 gives all 32 tokens, with logits 1.44e-2 apart after its router flips. The long prompt gives 28 of 32 tokens before the first difference.
- **Split across two Sparks over RDMA** (`2026-11-17-glm53/two/golden.rank0-gx10-02.log`): the same counts to the last choice (849,505 expert choices and 3,082,945 pools shared), so the split adds no difference of its own. The long prompt took 42 s instead of 63 s.

## Narrowing it down

**Not the GPU kernels.** Q8_0 matmuls on f32 tiled kernels instead of f16 tensor cores (`TF_MM_KERNEL=2`, `accuracy/golden-mm2.log`) give the same shares. Earlier, CPU experts against GPU experts gave logits within 4.1e-4.

**Not DSA selection.** With `TF_GOLDEN_DETAIL=1` (`accuracy/golden-detail.log`), the long prompt's first flipped choices come at token 19 by layer 21, long before any query has more than 2,048 tokens to choose from. At every flip, the engine's own gap between its 8th and 9th expert is below about 1e-5, sometimes exactly 0.

**Engine against itself.** The default and f32-kernel runs, compared with each other (`TF_GOLDEN_DUMP`, `accuracy/selfcmp.py`): 99.846% of expert choices and 99.968% of pools shared on the long prompt, against 97.2% and 99.71% with transformers.

**transformers against itself.** The reference rerun with 7 threads instead of 20, prompts only (`accuracy/reference_t7.log`, compared by `tools/ref/cmp_golden.py`): every expert choice and every DSA pool identical on all four prompts, logits 1.4e-5 to 3.5e-5 apart. TASK-31's same test on Qwen3.8 gave 99.642%. Either the engine has a systematic difference, or changing thread counts barely moves transformers' sums and the test is too gentle; the sensitivity test below decides which.

**Where it starts.** Hidden states, prompt 0, largest difference per layer (`accuracy/golden-hidden.log`, `accuracy/ref_vs_ref_hidden.py`):

| Layer | Engine vs transformers | transformers 20 vs 7 threads | Ratio | Largest value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 9.3e-8 | 2.2e-8 | 4 | 0.046 |
| 2 | 4.3e-7 | 1.2e-7 | 3.6 | 0.29 |
| 3 | 2.2e-5 | 7.2e-7 | 31 | 2.6 |
| 10 | 1.1e-4 | 2.2e-6 | 50 | 9.3 |
| 18 | 7.0e-4 | 9.5e-6 | 74 | 24 |
| 29 | 2.6e-3 | 2.9e-5 | 91 | 26 |
| 44 | 3.0e-2 | 1.8e-4 | 166 | 393 |

Layers 0 to 2 (dense, KDA) are ordinary f32 differences. Layer 3, the first with MoE experts and MLA attention, is where the ratio jumps.

**Inside layer 3.** Each part's input and output, engine (`TF_DUMP_LAYER=3`) against transformers (`ref_glm53.py --dump-layer 3`, `accuracy/reference_l3.log`), relative to the part's largest value (`accuracy/cmp_layer3.py`):

| Part | Prompt 0 | Prompt 1 | Prompt 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Layer 2 output (layer 3's input) | 1.5e-6 | | |
| Attention input, after the mHC mix and RMSNorm | 2.1e-5 | 5.0e-5 | 1.8e-5 |
| Attention output (MLA) | 6.4e-5 | 3.7e-5 | 5.1e-5 |
| MoE input | 2.3e-5 | 5.7e-5 | 5.4e-5 |
| Routed experts' output | 5.7e-6 | 1.4e-5 | 1.8e-5 |
| Shared expert's output | 6.8e-6 | 8.2e-6 | 5.5e-6 |

The experts are clean (their outputs are no worse than their inputs). The difference grows before attention runs, in the mHC step that mixes the 4 residual streams.

**The mHC input step in float64** (`accuracy/check_hc_pre.py`): computed from transformers' own layer 2 output with the GGUF's weights, it matches transformers within 3e-8 (with `rms_norm_eps` 1e-5 from `config.json`), so the engine's formula is right. Random noise of 1e-7 of the largest input value comes out about 16 times larger, so the step is ill-conditioned: the engine's 4e-7 at layer 2 explains its 2e-6 at layer 3's attention input without any error in the step itself.

**GLM is this sensitive.** A third reference run nudged the embeddings by random noise of 1e-6, about the engine's own difference at layer 0 (`ref_glm53.py --perturb 1e-6`, `accuracy/run_reference_p6.ps1`, `accuracy/reference_p6.log`, Nov 18 01:56 to 03:52). Compared with the plain run (`tools/ref/cmp_golden.py`, `accuracy/cmp_p6.log`):

| | Perturbed transformers vs plain | Engine vs plain |
|---|---|---|
| Short prompts, expert choices | 100% (logits 1.1e-5 to 1.9e-5) | 100%, 100%, 5,710 of 5,712 |
| Long prompt, expert choices | 97.943% | 97.242% |
| Long prompt, DSA pools | 99.795% | 99.708% |
| Long prompt, top-1 | 32 of 32 | 32 of 32 |

transformers itself, moved by noise the size of an ordinary f32 difference, lands within a whisker of where the engine lands on the long prompt. The thread-count test was simply too gentle. So the long prompt's shares measure the model's sensitivity, not an engine error, and the 99.5% and 99.9% targets cannot be met by any f32 implementation. Prompt 1's two flips (own gap 5.6e-5) are the one result the noise test does not reproduce.

## Amended criteria (Nov 18)

The user chose to amend TASK-32's criteria to this noise floor (`Plan.md`, TASK-32):

- **Long prompt:** at least 97% of expert choices and 99.6% of DSA pools shared, just under the noise run, with the first DSA layer and every top-1 identical. Its generated tokens are reported, not held (the noise run also starts the generation from a different state).
- **Short prompts:** a flip excuses the logits when the first layer with one flipped where the engine's own gap is below 1e-4. Later flips at the same token follow from it: prompt 1's second flip, at layer 41, has an own gap of 1.15e-3 because layer 40 already chose differently. Its tokens must still match, forward and generated.

`olmoe_run.zig` checks these for GLM-5.3-Flash only (models with DSA). Qwen3.8's QSA thresholds are unchanged, and the engine's own gaps are now kept on every golden run, not only with `TF_GOLDEN_DETAIL`. One process on `gx10-02` (`accuracy/golden-amended.log`) passes: short-prompt logits within 2.27e-3 apart from prompt 1's excused 5.05e-2, every top-1 identical, the long prompt at 97.242% and 99.708%, generation 32 of 32 tokens on every short prompt and 28 of 32 on the long one.

## The two-Spark window (Nov 17, 23:42 to Nov 18, 00:10)

This window's logs are in `2026-11-17-glm53/two/nov17/`.

`gx10-03`'s owner was using it (their server stopped by them, a build and a large download running), so the run used `gx10-02` and `gx10-01`, with `gx10-01` holding experts 144 to 287 (`stage.py --add 144 191` added 28.5 GB to its three-Spark share in 2 minutes). The desktop was left out: 64 GB of memory, partly in use by the reference, and its experts would run on the CPU over TCP.

- **Tiny rehearsal:** PASS over RDMA with one peer.
- **Golden:** as above, identical to one process.
- **Chat:** failed on the first decode step: a non-finite GPU output from one expert call on `gx10-01` (layer 12, expert 177), where the CPU gave 0.216 from the same weights.
- **32k and 128k:** no result. The expert rank on `gx10-01` had been killed (below), so 32k waited on a dead peer until its 20-minute cap, and 128k was stopped by hand.

**What went wrong.** `window2.sh` reaches `gx10-01` through `gx10-02` (`ssh gx10-02 ssh admin@...`), and the second hop lost the command's quoting: the `(nohup flock ... sleep 10800 &)` that holds `gx10-01`'s watchdog lock failed on a syntax error, while plain commands such as `docker stop` went through. The watchdog counted the stopped container as unhealthy each minute and restarted `qwen38-27b-tf` at 23:48, beside our 85 GB expert rank. At 23:51 the OOM killer took our rank and the cluster pods `velero`, two `loki-canary` and `node_exporter`; Kubernetes restarted them within minutes, and the lab server was healthy afterwards. The window's final check reported the lock as still held, which was the watchdog's own per-minute run holding it for a moment. The chat's non-finite value came 48 seconds after the container restarted, so memory pressure may be its cause; that is not shown.

**Fixed since:**
- Commands to `gx10-01` and `gx10-03` go through helpers that quote them again for the second hop (`printf %q`), in `window.sh` and `window2.sh`; the lock, its release and the container check were tested on `gx10-01`.
- `window2.sh` confirms the lock is held before anything loads, and stops if not.
- `window.sh` refuses to start when either lab container is not running (its owner may be using the machine some other way), and starts again only what it stopped.
- A non-finite GPU expert output is replaced by the CPU's result for the same call and counted (`split.zig`, `checkCall`), as decided for TASK-31's intermittent case.

A rerun of chat, 32k and 128k was set to wait for `gx10-01`'s server to have no new requests for 15 minutes, from 04:00 and starting no later than 05:30, with everything restored by 07:00 (`when_idle.sh`, `two-idle.log`). The server took about 8 requests every 5 minutes all morning, so the waiter was stopped at 04:50 without touching `gx10-01`, and the run moves to another day.

## The second two-Spark window (Nov 20, 03:50 to 06:17)

`gx10-01`'s server was idle (GPU at 0%, no requests in 6 hours), so the user said to run the window then, with everything restored by 08:00. Same split as before: `gx10-02` as rank 0 with experts 0 to 143, `gx10-01` with 144 to 287, every expert in memory (95.8 GB and 85.6 GB resident), builds `zig-out-g32m` and `bin/olmoe-g32m`. Logs are in `2026-11-17-glm53/two/`, the console in `two-console3.log`.

- **Watchdog:** the lock was confirmed held before anything loaded, and the watchdog logged no event during the window.
- **Tiny rehearsal:** PASS over RDMA.
- **Chat:** the three turns are coherent (`two/chat.txt`) at 4.2 tokens/s decoding, against 2.3 to 2.4 over three Sparks on Nov 17 before the speedups. First tokens came in 2.0 s, 1.5 s (116 of 137 prompt tokens reused) and 13.0 s (259 tokens). No non-finite GPU output on either rank. The recording is `two/chat.jsonl.gz`; with every expert in memory it has no cache or prefetch data.
- **32k (complete):** time to first token 1,410 s (23 tokens/s), then 3.52 tokens/s decoding (0.284 s per token) over 127 steps from position 32,640, at 99.5 GB resident on rank 0. Prefill time: experts 936 s, attention 309 s, KDA 134 s. 5,519,891 expert calls went to `gx10-01` (run in 6,010 grouped jobs, 68 s of GPU time there) and 5,489,821 ran on `gx10-02`. On Nov 17, three Sparks reached only 4,096 positions in 1,074 s (3.8 tokens/s).
- **128k (no result):** prefill held 21 to 23 tokens/s up to 79,872 positions (3,574 s), then hung. For 35 minutes both ranks' main threads spun at 100% with no voluntary context switches while both GPUs and every worker thread sat idle, so each side was waiting on the other over RDMA. The router was stopped at 05:52. perf and ptrace are blocked on the shared account, so the cause was found by reading the code (below), not from the hung processes.

**The 128k hang.** The tiny model (vocabulary-sized copy of the same tokens) runs 131,040 positions in one process and split over RDMA without hanging, so long positions alone do not hang the compute; the expert's main thread busy-polls, so its 100% was idle waiting. The transport has a deadlock that fits: `Endpoint.pump` polled only as many completions as its 256-entry inbox had room for, and RDMA rings have 256 slots. While the GPU expert sends a gathered batch's answers, each send pumps rank 0's new calls into its inbox without reading them; once 256 wait there it stops polling completions at all, so rank 0's credit grants go unseen and the expert waits for credits forever, while rank 0 waits for the expert to take its calls. It needs the batch to be flushed while rank 0 is still dispatching the same layer (a 200 us lull mid-layer), which is a timing race: once in about 14 million calls here. A unit test (`transport.zig`, "credits still arrive while the inbox is full of unread packets") fails on the old code exactly so. Fixed: `pump` always polls, a data packet arriving to a full inbox is a protocol error (credits keep unread packets within `slots`), and `--slots` above 256 is refused. With the fix (`zig-out-g32p` on `gx10-02`) the tiny golden passes in one process, over shared memory and over RDMA, and the tests pass. The same race also stalled every batch without hanging: the tiny model's 128k over RDMA on `gx10-02` took 504.7 s to its first token on the old build, with the expert's grouped jobs averaging 1.74 s, and takes 9.0 s on the fixed one, with jobs of 366 us (the same 1,043,881 calls). A batch stalled until its inbox happened to drop below full; in the real 128k run it stayed full. The real model's 32k jobs averaged 11.4 ms (max 187 ms), so its speed with the fix needs measuring too. A 128k run over two Sparks with the fix needs another window; logs of these checks are in `2026-11-17-glm53/hang/`.

**How the window ran.** The first 32k hit `run.sh`'s 20-minute cap at 26,624 positions (`two/32k-capped.*`). To let both long prompts finish, 128k's `timeout` was paused (`SIGSTOP` on the timer only, the router kept running) and a follow-on script on `gx10-02` (`after128.log`) waited for 128k, stopped its expert rank, ran 32k with a 40-minute cap, and then resumed the timer so `window2.sh` restored `gx10-01`. A second lock holder was queued for when the first one's 3 hours ran out. `gx10-01`'s server was healthy at 06:17:34 and the lock was released at 06:17:36, with none of our processes left there.

## Tools added

- `TF_GOLDEN_DETAIL=1`: per layer, the expert choices that differ, the first token, the engine's smallest own gap there, and each layer's hidden-state difference.
- `TF_GOLDEN_DUMP=PREFIX`: each prompt's expert choices and DSA pools as raw files, to compare two engine runs (`accuracy/selfcmp.py`).
- `TF_DUMP_LAYER=L`: layer L's attention and MoE inputs and outputs (GLM-5.3-Flash only).
- `tools/ref/cmp_golden.py`: two goldens of the same prompts compared.
- `ref_glm53.py`: `--threads`, `--dump-layer L` and `--perturb EPS`.
