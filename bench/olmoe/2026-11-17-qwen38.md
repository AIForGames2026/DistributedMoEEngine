# Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on one DGX Spark, experts streamed from NVMe (TASK-31, 2026-11-17)

The engine now runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (transformers' `qwen4_exp`, GGUF architecture `qwen4exp`) from ggml-org's Q8_0 GGUF: 163 GB in two parts, SHA-256 in `versions.txt`. That is more than `gx10-02`'s 121 GB of memory, so the experts stream from NVMe through the expert cache (TASK-11, TASK-14).

The model, as checked against its `config.json` and transformers 5.23's `modeling_qwen4_exp.py`:

- **48 layers**, hidden size 2,560:
  - three Gated DeltaNet layers (TASK-29's, with a sigmoid output gate) to every Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA) layer;
  - 36 and 12 layers in all.
- **QSA:** an indexer scores blocks of 4 tokens and each query keeps the best 512 blocks (2,048 tokens), plus the unfinished last block. Below 2,048 tokens of context, it is plain attention.
- **Hyper-connections:** the residual is 4 streams. A low-rank gate mixes them into each block's input, and the block's output is added back to each stream with its own weight.
- **N-gram embedding:**
  - At layer 1, each token's bigram and trigram hash into 16 tables of about 20 million rows.
  - The rows are 28.8 GB in Q4_0, held in memory.
  - They are gated per stream and passed through a short dilated convolution.
- **MoE:** 512 experts of 640 (2.5 MB each in Q8_0, 128 GB in all), 10 routed plus a gated shared expert.

What changed:

- **Model:**
  - `src/qwen38.zig` holds the hyper-connections, the n-gram embedding and QSA. `olmoe.step` calls them.
  - The loader takes a split GGUF: pass the `-00002-of-00002` part, which holds the tensors, and part 1's metadata is merged in.
  - Q4_0 is a new weight format.
- **GPU:** three QSA kernels: block scores, top-k selection, and attention over the kept blocks. Each QSA pass ran on the GPU.
- **Expert cache and lookahead:** lookahead now predicts through the hyper-connections, and still leaves every logit bit-identical. The cache is unchanged.
- **NVMe-tiered KV cache:** `--kv-offload` now covers hybrid models, keeping each Gated DeltaNet layer's state in memory. It is bit-identical on the GPU tiny model.
- **Chat format:** `qwen38` matches the model's template.
  - Every assistant turn keeps its reasoning.
  - The generation prompt opens a `<think>` block, so the server starts the reply on the reasoning channel.
  - `chat_template_kwargs.reasoning_effort` takes `xhigh` (the template's default), `medium` or `low`.
  - Requests the template refuses (a system message after the first, or no user message) get a 400.
- **Diagnostics:** see "Non-finite values" below.
- **Reference:** `tools/ref/ref_qwen38.py` runs transformers' own `Qwen4ExpForCausalLM` on the GGUF's dequantized weights.
  - It dequantizes only the expert and n-gram rows a prompt uses, since the whole model in float32 would not fit in memory.
  - QSA ties go to the lower block, as in the engine. torch leaves their order unspecified, and the reference counts them (0 of 64,536 here).

Raw logs, the scripts that ran them, and versions are in [`2026-11-17-qwen38/`](2026-11-17-qwen38/).

Setup:

- `gx10-02`: kernel 7.0.0-1019-nvidia, driver 580.178.04, Zig 0.17.0, with nothing else running.
- Engine build `v0.2.0-alpha-42-g1b78bb4-q38k`.
- Reference: transformers 5.23.0, torch 2.15.0, CPU, float32, eager attention. The golden file took about 30 minutes and 115 GB on `gx10-02`'s CPU, so it never ran beside the engine.
- Expert cache: `--gpu --cache 12000`, 12,000 slots (63 GB) with 2 loader threads. Cached experts run on the GPU, in place in the Spark's shared memory.

## Against transformers (TASK-10 and TASK-24 checks)

There are four prompts:

- the three TASK-24 prompts (5, 19 and 15 tokens);
- a 2,600-token prompt, long enough that QSA chooses blocks. It is the start of `Plan.md` as it stood on Nov 16, kept as `long_prompt.txt` on `gx10-02`.

Each prompt is followed by 32 greedy tokens. The long prompt is held to the criterion amended on Nov 16 (`Plan.md`, TASK-31):

- QSA's first layer that chooses blocks gives identical choices;
- at least 99.9% of QSA blocks and 99.5% of expert choices are shared;
- every top-1 token is identical.

The reason is that choosing 512 of about 650 blocks per query amplifies float rounding through the layers. transformers rerun with 7 threads instead of 20 shares only 99.912% of its own QSA blocks and 99.642% of its expert choices, with logits 1.96 apart (`reference-7-threads.log`).

| Run | Short prompts: worst logits error | Short prompts: routing | Long prompt: QSA blocks | Long prompt: experts | Top-1 | Generated tokens equal to `generate()` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| One process, GPU | 9.73e-5 | 18,720 of 18,720 | 99.928% | 99.698% | 71 of 71 | 128 of 128 |
| Two processes, shared memory | 9.73e-5 | 18,720 of 18,720 | 99.928% | 99.698% | 71 of 71 | 128 of 128 |

- **Both pass** (`golden-gpu.log`, `split-shm.log`).
  - The long prompt's first QSA layer (layer 3) chose identically.
  - The two runs gave identical numbers throughout.
  - The 12 QSA passes over the long prompt ran on the GPU.
- **The split** sends activations in f32 (`--payload f32`), and each process has half the cache (6,000 slots). Rank 1 ran 1,270,833 of the 2,592,960 expert calls, at 63 µs each.

**Router near-ties end the logit check, not the token check.** In two of the generations, transformers' router has a near-tie, where the 10th and 11th expert probabilities differ by less than 1e-6:

- prompt 0, step 13: gap 8.38e-9;
- prompt 3, step 8: gap 3.59e-8.

Either expert is a valid choice, and the logits after it can move by up to 0.63. So the logits are held to 1e-2 up to the near-tie, and the tokens are held all the way. Every generated token matches. Before the near-ties, the short prompts' generations match within 8.5e-5.

**Repeats.** Earlier the same day the long prompt ran three more times, plus two runs that verified every expert load (`repeats.log`).

- All gave the same numbers, to the last digit.
- A run with every GPU matrix product rechecked on the CPU (`TF_GPU_CHECK=1`, 46 minutes) also passed.

## Long context on `gx10-02`

**The prompt:**

- It is the repository's own text, tokenized with the model's tokenizer: `repo.tokens`, 526,324 tokens from 88 files.
- It is prefilled in 2,048-token chunks with `--max-step 2048`, then decoded greedily.
- The KV cache is f32 and in memory. Only the 12 QSA layers keep keys and values: 49,152 bytes per token, plus each Gated DeltaNet layer's fixed state.

| Context | KV cache | First token | Prefill | Decode | Peak memory | Wall time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32,640 + 128 | 1.61 GB | 1,210.7 s | 27 tokens/s | 5.71 tokens/s | 98.4 GB | 20 min 57 s |
| 131,040 + 32 | 6.44 GB | 4,970.9 s | 26 tokens/s | 4.22 tokens/s | 103.9 GB | 1 h 23 min |

**The expert cache:**

| Context | Expert calls | Already loaded | Hinted, still loading | Missed | Loads | Read from NVMe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32,640 | 15.7 million | 90.0% | 4.8% | 5.2% | 331,321 (2.17 ms each) | 1,730 GB |
| 131,040 | 62.9 million | 90.9% | 4.0% | 5.2% | 1,295,767 (2.15 ms each) | 6,767 GB |

**Prefill speed holds roughly steady from 32k to 128k** (27 and 26 tokens/s), because the experts set it:

- **32k:** experts took 917 s of the 1,211 s.
- **128k:** experts took 3,744 s of the 4,971 s.
- **Attention is small by comparison:** 96 s at 128k, against 1,074 s for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B before its tensor-core kernel. QSA attends to at most 2,048 tokens per query.
- **NVMe reads:** a 2,048-token chunk uses nearly all 24,576 experts, about twice what the 12,000 slots hold. So each chunk reloads about 20,000 of them: 6.8 TB read from NVMe over the 128k prompt.

**Decode:**

- Experts are 45% of decode time at 32k and 58% at 128k.
- Between 32k and 128k, speed falls from 5.71 to 4.22 tokens/s.
  - Nearly all of that drop is in the experts, which went from 80 to 135 ms per step.
  - Attention held at about 31 ms per step.
  - The 128k run decoded only 31 tokens, a small sample.

Nothing here is tuned for this model yet. More cache slots, or the n-gram table read from NVMe to free 29 GB for them, are the obvious next steps.

## Non-finite values in two early runs

Twice, in about 13 runs of the GPU golden check on earlier builds, the long prompt's prefill produced a non-finite value.

- The first diverged after layer 15. Its log was not kept.
- The second stopped after layer 17 on the guard added after the first (`nonfinite.log`): `step()` now fails with `error.NonFinite` and names the layer, rather than reporting garbage.
- The first came right after a reference run. In the kept log, the three short prompts before the long one passed.

**What it is not:**

- **Not overflow:** values in that layer normally stay below about 26.
- **Not a slot freed under a running call:** the GPU expert calls finish before their cache slot is released.

**Not yet ruled out:** a bad expert load. The two repeats that checked every load's CRC32C were clean, but neither hit the fault.

**What was added to catch it next time** (in `q38k`):

- **Every expert call's output is checked for finite values.** A bad one is recomputed on the CPU from the same cache slot, and both are printed with the input's range.
- **The guard's message counts the bad rows** in the expert input, the expert output and the residual, and names the first token.
- **`TF_NAN_TRACE=1`** prints each layer's ranges, and flags any expert output of 1e4 or more.

**Since then:**

- 20 consecutive runs of the GPU golden check with the trace on (`nan-trace-loop.sh`, `nan-trace-loop.log`) flagged nothing and finished cleanly.
- So did every `q38k` run without the trace: the golden check, the split, 32k, 128k, and the chat recording.

The trace changes timing, which fits a race, but chance cannot be ruled out at these rates. Like TASK-15b's one differing round, it stays unexplained. If it recurs, the per-call check will name the expert, the token and the slot.

## Tokenizer and chat format

**The tokenizer** is TASK-29's `qwen35` pre-tokenizer.

- On 528 texts it gives exactly Hugging Face's ids (75,997 tokens), loaded from the GGUF and from `tokenizer.json` alike (`tools/ref/check_tokenizer.py`).

**The chat format** was checked with `tools/ref/check_chat.py`.

- **Method:** the engine renders the same conversations as transformers' `apply_chat_template` with the GGUF's own template, across thinking on and off and `reasoning_effort` low and medium.
- **qwen38:** 108 of 108 prompts are identical. The 12 conversations the template rejects are refused by both.
- **Qwen3, rechecked with its own GGUF template:** 108 of 108.

## Tests

`zig build test`: 97 of 97 on WSL2. New tests:

- **The tiny Qwen3.8 model against transformers** (`ref_qwen38.py --tiny`): it has the layer mix, a QSA budget small enough to select, hyper-connections and the n-gram embedding. Every layer's output, the logits, routing, QSA blocks, and the generated tokens with their logits match within 1e-4, and a prompt fed in two pieces gives the same logits. `tests/golden/qwen38_gpu.*`, the same model with 256-wide heads, is for checking the GPU kernels on a Spark, outside `zig build test`.
- **Lookahead on hyper-connections:** it leaves every logit bit-identical.
- **The n-gram hash:** it starts over after an end-of-text token.
- **Q4_0:** blocks decode in ggml's nibble order.
- **The `qwen38` chat format:** it keeps every turn's reasoning and opens the reply's.
- **The server:** a prompt that opened the reasoning starts the reply on that channel.

## Not done

- **A split across two Sparks with every expert in memory:** this needs a second free Spark (`gx10-01` and `gx10-03` run other people's servers).
- **Prompt reuse:** as for Qwen3.6, the server cannot reuse a prompt prefix with this model, since a Gated DeltaNet state cannot step back. Each chat turn prefills the whole conversation, as the replay's logs show (0 tokens reused).
- **`--prefix-cache`:** it still refuses hybrid models.
